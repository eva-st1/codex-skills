# Public copy review

## Review in visitor order

Start with the first screen and primary action, then navigation/contact routes, offer details, proof, pricing, FAQ, metadata, and secondary pages. Focus a narrow request on its actual scope.

Ask whether a visitor can identify the business or product, what it offers, who it serves, and the next useful action. Mention location when it affects eligibility or discovery; it is not necessary in every headline.

## Meaning and credibility

- Match positioning, visible facts, metadata, and structured descriptions.
- Use trust cues supported by real evidence. A badge shape does not make a claim credible.
- Make pricing, inclusions, exclusions, and qualification conditions clear when known.
- Answer real customer concerns in FAQs instead of repeating marketing claims.
- Preserve a sourced first-person commitment when it carries the business's distinctive offer.
- Prefer a confirmed start date over a rolling experience count when either communicates the same fact.
- Keep necessary legal, accessibility, service-process, and consent information readable.

## Visible wording

Use one main point per heading, card, or CTA. Keep headings compact enough for the layout, without a fixed word-count rule. Subheads should sharpen meaning rather than echo the headline.

Remove decorative eyebrow stacks, unexplained category labels, repeated closing sections, and badges whose values merely repeat their labels when they add no useful information. Question headings can work for genuine questions; choose the form that fits the business and content.

Contact copy should explain what happens next or give a verified commitment. It should not narrate a mail client or repeat the whole hero. CTAs must accurately describe the available action, especially when booking, payment, or submission has consequences.

Keep audit notes, missing-proof comments, implementation details, and source citations used only for internal verification outside final public copy. Preserve attribution when readers need it to assess a material claim.

## Multilingual review

Compare language versions against the same factual intent. Natural syntax, paragraph shape, and CTA idiom may differ. Check that changes in price, scope, availability, or eligibility reach all relevant variants. Do not propagate a language-only edit as a new business promise.

Review translated navigation, mobile menu labels, forms, validation messages, consent text, metadata, and proof captions as well as paragraphs. Use proper locale characters and formatting. Language-selector design belongs to the site's interaction system; do not prescribe a new selector solely to complete a copy task.

## Rendered check

Inspect headline wraps, clipped buttons, card-height pressure, title/value repetition, visual reading order, and contact expectations on relevant viewport sizes. For Polish typography, avoid stranded one-letter function words where the rendering system supports it; apply non-breaking behavior in presentation rather than silently altering source facts.

Document any limit to the review: text-only input, missing mobile view, unavailable contact destination, or unconfirmed claim. Do not describe an unrendered source edit as visually verified.
