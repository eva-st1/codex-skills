# English website copy

Establish the target variety of English from the audience, market, brand, and supplied text. Preserve British, American, or another requested convention consistently.

Lead with the concrete offer: what changes, what the visitor gets, and what they can do next. Prefer active verbs and specific nouns. Match warmth and detail to the business; an artist portfolio, enterprise product, and local service need different voices.

Review phrases such as `seamless`, `unlock`, `empower`, `game-changing`, and `tailored solutions` for actual meaning. Replace filler with a capability, scope, condition, or next step rather than applying a mechanical banned-word list.

Useful CTA examples include `Request a quote`, `Book a visit`, `Send the brief`, and `See selected work`. Choose the action that actually follows. Use `Call now` only where immediacy is appropriate and a working phone route exists.

Do not mirror source-language word order or import legal phrasing from another market. Retain verified terminology and necessary qualifications. FAQ answers should answer first, with a caveat when needed. Metadata should describe the page naturally rather than chain keywords.

Check spelling variety, currency/units, address conventions, contractions, formality, and the brand's singular/plural voice across the entire flow.
