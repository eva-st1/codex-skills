# Website copywriting

Help a visitor understand the offer, judge its credibility, and take a useful next step. Preserve the business's voice and verified facts. Match the amount of copy to the evidence and the visitor's decision.

## Establish the work

Choose drafting, revision, localization, review, or implementation from the request. A critique authorizes inspection and recommendations; requested edits should be made in the owning source or document when available.

Use supplied briefs, existing pages, brand guidance, business information, screenshots, or source files. A dedicated content platform, CLI, database, or repository is not required. In a repository, inspect dirty state, read applicable instructions, locate the owning page/template, and follow its normal editing and verification workflow.

Before drafting, establish a compact audience frame:

- who will read this and what brought them here;
- what they already know;
- the decision or action in front of them;
- the likely concern or hesitation;
- facts that can help them decide;
- tone, detail, and next step appropriate to that moment.

Infer this from available evidence and ask only when ambiguity would materially change the work. Keep the frame in working notes unless the user requests strategy.

## Sources, voice, and language

Read relevant existing copy before replacing it. Preserve strong offer wording and distinctive commitments. Maintain a small claim-to-source map for material promises, pricing, qualifications, reviews, scope, availability, and outcomes. User-confirmed business information is usable evidence; clarify contradictions or missing support rather than inventing proof.

Identify language/market, formality, address mode, brand voice, CTA intent, terms to preserve, and terms to avoid. These are editorial decisions, not mandatory schema fields. Explicit brand and user preferences govern over generic locale defaults.

Load [public copy review](references/public-copy-review.md) for a full drafting or review pass. Load only the relevant language references:

- [Polish](references/pl.md)
- [English](references/en.md)

For other languages, use supplied voice evidence and locale-appropriate language judgment; do not force one of these markets onto the task.

## Draft and edit

Lead with the actual offer, audience relevance, and a clear next step. Use concrete nouns and verbs. Headings should name useful content; CTAs should make the next action clear and match what the destination delivers.

Keep public copy in the business's voice. Remove internal audit language, source-provenance notes, design rationale, placeholder instructions, and explanations of the page or button mechanics. Preserve necessary customer-facing explanations of the actual service process, consent, accessibility, and legal terms.

Never invent testimonials, credentials, guarantees, response times, discounts, capacity, metrics, or customer preferences. If evidence is thin, use a smaller truthful page rather than more generic sections. Label unresolved draft facts outside publishable copy, and use a clearly visible placeholder only when the user needs a working draft.

For localization, preserve intent and facts rather than sentence order. Review every locale carrying a shared factual change; keep a language-specific rewrite scoped when that is what the user requested. Confirm idiom, formality, CTA meaning, terminology, prices/units, and contact routes in each variant.

## Verify and deliver

Read copy in its actual context where possible: first screen, navigation, cards, proof, pricing, FAQ, contact flow, and mobile view. Check wrapping, truncation, label/value pairs, CTA destinations, and agreement between visible copy and metadata. Source inspection alone does not prove rendered quality; state when screenshots or browser access are unavailable.

For implementation, run the owning project's focused checks and inspect affected pages. Do not introduce regex tests that claim to prove tone, honesty, or idiom. For a text-only request, provide the requested copy without requiring a site build.

Lead with the revision or most consequential findings. For reviews, identify the page/section, observed problem, user impact, and a concrete rewrite or evidence request. For edits, report changed files and checks actually performed. Publishing and account changes follow the user's authorization and existing workflow.
