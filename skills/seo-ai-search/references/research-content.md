# Research, content, and local discovery

## Query and competitor research

Combine the buyer's vocabulary with Search Console queries, product/service pages, customer questions, and observed search results. Keep country, language, device, and observation date alongside SERP evidence. General web search is not a location-specific rank tracker; report its limitations.

For each query/cluster, record intent, audience need, current target URL, observed result format/features, available performance data, and a decision: improve, create, consolidate, investigate, or defer. Use vendor difficulty only if supplied or retrieved. Explain feasibility through actual competition, content fit, evidence, and effort rather than a made-up probability.

Inspect competitor pages for unanswered needs and useful formats. Coverage is not proof of rankings. State a ranking gap only with comparable rank data; otherwise describe an observed content gap. Traffic opportunity requires a named dataset or explicit model. Choose opportunity counts from evidence, not fixed quotas.

## Page optimization and answer sections

Inspect page purpose and main content before editing titles or adding questions. Check descriptive titles, useful headings, clear opening answers, metadata, image context/alt text, navigable links, and query alignment. Write titles/descriptions for clarity; preview truncation instead of enforcing universal character cutoffs.

For a snippet or answer opportunity, inspect the current result format when possible. Draft a direct answer, steps, table, or comparison when it helps the reader. Preserve qualifications, units, evidence, and product truth. Give exact placement and paste-ready copy or a repository edit. Concise answers are an editorial choice, not a promise of snippet capture or AI inclusion.

Evaluate trust with specific evidence: real author/reviewer expertise where relevant, original examples/testing, sources supporting important claims, accurate update dates, business identity/contact details, and commercial disclosures. Report missing signals and concrete fixes. E-E-A-T is a quality framework; a homemade score is not a Google metric.

## Topic coverage and briefs

Map existing URLs to audience questions and journey stages. Inspect substance and links before declaring coverage. A sitemap lists candidates; it does not prove quality, indexing, or ranking.

Use hub/supporting-page structures where distinct pages serve distinct needs. Improve existing pages before creating overlapping content. A brief identifies the audience question, target URL, format, proposed sections, differentiating evidence needed, sources, internal links, and useful next action. Avoid mandatory word counts, page counts, or schema density.

For pages competing on the same intent, investigate performance and content differences before recommending a merge, redirect, or canonical change. Map the destination and user journey.

## Local discovery

Apply to businesses with a real local/service-area presence. Establish actual business name, service area, contact details, hours, category, services, and Business Profile evidence. Mark account-only fields unverified when access is unavailable.

Compare factual consistency on the website, profile, and relevant local/industry directories. Prioritize wrong information and missing valuable sources for that market; a US directory list is not universal. Use real locations, useful service information, and authentic review handling. Never invent reviews or add keywords to a business name the business does not use.

Local-pack positions require observed queries and location context. Recommend specific profile/page corrections and verification evidence; do not infer local visibility from ordinary organic results.
