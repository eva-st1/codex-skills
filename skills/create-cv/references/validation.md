# CV validation

Review the checks relevant to the requested deliverable. A successful render cannot compensate for false content; good text alone cannot prove document layout.

## Facts and candidate instructions

Trace substantive claims to a supplied source or clear candidate account. Check names, employers, titles, dates, metrics, qualifications, tools, languages, and ownership. Resolve material contradictions. Remove unsupported causality, precision, seniority, or proficiency.

Apply active corrections and preferences, with the latest instruction controlling in its scope. Compare with the accepted baseline when available. Explain material deletions; confirm that compression has not removed the evidence that justified the application. Do not describe a rejected revision as candidate-approved.

## Relevance and writing

Check whether the first page shows the intended role and strongest relevant proof. Important requirements should be covered or honestly treated as gaps. Review specific contributions, language quality, diacritics, terminology, tense, and dates. Avoid duplicated summaries, generic traits, and keyword padding.

Apply a separate native-language editorial pass when translation quality warrants it. This may be the same agent's explicit review; a separate person or service is not mandatory. Do not claim an external review occurred if it did not.

## Market, privacy, and instructions

Check actual employer requirements, language, document type, page limit, and anonymization. Keep personal data minimal and intentional. Do not supply referee contact details without permission. Verify current official guidance before asserting a legal requirement or drafting legal wording.

Check the employer's rules for AI assistance and assessments when disclosed. Do not imply that the candidate used an unaided process if that claim is false or prohibited assistance was used.

## Extraction and format

Use a conservative primary layout: one column, clear section headings, ordinary text for essential contact information, simple bullets, and consistent dates. Essential content should not depend on images, tables, text boxes, headers, or footers. These are parsing precautions, not a guarantee about every hiring system; see [Greenhouse's documented parsing issues](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse).

For PDF, extract text with `pdftotext` or an available equivalent. For other formats, inspect the document's reading order using appropriate tools. Compare extracted text with the intended content: roles, employers, dates, contact routes, headings, representative accented characters, and bullets. Check for omissions, duplication, fragmented words, and reordered columns. Confirm required type and size limits from the actual application channel.

Successful extraction proves technical readability in that test, not screening success. Do not invent a commercial ATS-match score or treat an AI-text detector as proof of authorship.

## Rendered review

When generating a document, inspect every page at readable resolution. Check clipping, overlap, blank pages, split role headers, orphaned headings, spacing, hierarchy, links, and legibility. Confirm that all intended content remains after pagination and that the output is a CV rather than a renderer error page.

Use the appropriate available document or PDF workflow. No specific library or additional file format is mandatory. If rendering is unavailable, provide the source or draft and disclose that visual QA remains unverified. Do not report checks that were not performed.

## Proof and handoff

For selected links, verify accessible destinations and factual agreement with the CV where tools permit it. Distinguish link checks from a full portfolio review. Remove or flag broken, misleading, or unpermissioned proof; respect candidate preferences when choosing a replacement.

Deliver the requested text or files, the meaningful checks performed, and any unresolved factual, format, or access issue. Keep working evidence, personal data, and career documents out of a public repository unless the user has expressly chosen publication. Preparation does not establish that an application was submitted.
