# Professional CV method

## Target and evidence map

For a specific vacancy, separate eligibility, core delivery, domain knowledge, tools/methods, collaboration, leadership, language, and location/authorization needs. Mark requirements as must, important, or supporting from the actual wording; distinguish judgment from an explicit employer requirement.

Use a compact table when useful:

`Requirement | Priority | Supporting source | Coverage | Risk or missing evidence`

Coverage can be strong, partial, absent, or unknown. A missing source is not proof the candidate lacks the capability. Do not disguise a gap by adding synonyms. For a general CV, state the intended role family and use its relevant capabilities without inventing a vacancy.

Prefer recent, directly relevant outcomes, then relevant scope or delivered work, honest transferable evidence, and relevant qualifications. For every substantive line, check that its source supports all modifiers, numbers, tools, dates, and implied ownership.

## Positioning and structure

Form an internal thesis: candidate's role, relevant capability, employer need, and strongest differentiating evidence. Use it to select and order content; do not paste the formula into the CV.

The first part of page one should communicate professional identity, target relevance, and credible proof. A concise summary can help a transition or non-obvious fit; omit it if it merely repeats the title and skills. Follow candidate preferences rather than enforcing a fixed summary length or card layout.

Use ordinary document text for the name, contact routes, and essential links. Include broad location or verified work eligibility only when useful. Follow employer anonymization instructions, and minimize optional personal details.

For experience, preserve employer, official title, dates, and the contribution boundary. Use reverse chronology, with the most relevant evidence prominent within each role. Keep enough context to understand the work. A useful bullet can combine action/contribution, problem, scope/method, and supported output/result; not every bullet needs all four parts.

Group concrete, relevant skills into readable categories. Connect important skills to actual roles, projects, education, or credentials. Do not infer proficiency from an occupational label or pad the list with every tool encountered.

Use official qualification names and truthful language levels. Add a translation or market clarification without claiming an unverified equivalence. Do not imply an expired credential is current.

Optional projects, publications, volunteering, awards, and courses earn space when they add relevant evidence or explain the profile. Label paid, academic, volunteer, and speculative work accurately.

## Writing

Use the requested language or the vacancy language when unspecified. Prefer precise verbs, concrete nouns, and supported scope to generic traits. Keep tense, dates, punctuation, spelling variety, and terminology consistent. Expand unfamiliar abbreviations without changing official names.

Translate meaning, not word order. Preserve qualification and job-title accuracy across languages. Do not replace a clear native phrase with an unnecessary English hybrid merely to sound professional.

## Revision and compression

Identify the evidence that directly supports the most important requirements and anything explicitly protected by candidate feedback. Compare the new version with the supplied baseline and latest accepted version when available.

Compress duplication, vague summaries, repeated tool lists, and irrelevant optional material before cutting substantive work evidence. Merge genuinely overlapping bullets without merging different contributions or outcomes. Keep dates, role continuity, and readable text.

Page count is a constraint to manage, not proof of quality. If a hard employer limit conflicts with requested content, explain the concrete tradeoff and offer the strongest compliant edit. If no hard limit exists, another readable page may be better than weakened evidence. Do not automatically convert a particular candidate's preferred photograph, summary length, or project count into a rule for everyone.

## Cross-surface consistency and proof

Where supplied or accessible, compare dates, titles, skill claims, contribution boundaries, and language levels across the CV, professional profile, application fields, and work samples. Resolve the underlying fact rather than making documents consistently wrong. Do not require account access for a useful CV draft.

A proof link should verify something material. The CV needs a concise statement of the candidate's contribution even if nobody opens it. Keep confidential work private; a sanitized case study requires permission and must still be truthful.

## Handoff notes

For substantial tailoring, retain enough working notes to explain requirement coverage, evidence selected/omitted, key wording choices, market assumptions, and remaining gaps. Existing provenance or revision systems can be used when present; hashes, private IDs, and scoring schemas are not prerequisites for a standalone CV.

Use a clear employer-facing filename, such as `<Name>_CV_<Company>_<Role>.pdf`, adapted to the user's naming preference and required format. Keep internal artifact IDs and private review notes out of the submitted document.
