# Portfolio and proof

## Choose a proportionate proof surface

Classify proof as required by the employer, expected for the occupation, useful differentiation, optional, or counterproductive because it is weak, irrelevant, inaccessible, or confidential. A portfolio is not automatically a website: a PDF, repository, published writing, analysis, demo, or single strong sample may be enough.

Map each proposed link:

`Employer need | CV claim | Deeper proof | Candidate contribution | Sharing permission`

Select complementary evidence rather than a large archive. The CV must work on its own; the linked surface should add evidence instead of repeating it in a larger layout.

## Make proof inspectable

A selected sample should explain the context/problem, personal role, meaningful constraints and decisions, delivered artifact, and supported result or learning. Distinguish individual, shared, and team contributions. An observable output can be useful without a numerical business outcome.

Use a flexible case study:

1. Brief context, role, timeframe, and output.
2. Why the problem mattered and what evidence was available.
3. Important decisions, alternatives, and tradeoffs.
4. Inspectable artifacts and their contribution boundary.
5. Supported results, limitations, and reflection.

Senior work often needs evidence of judgment, ambiguity, influence, and systems rather than a long catalogue of routine process steps. Label speculative exercises as exercises.

## Match proof to the role

| Role | Potential proof |
| --- | --- |
| Engineering | Source, deployed output, design decisions, tests, release or reliability evidence |
| Data/research | Question, data provenance, reproducible analysis, interpretation, uncertainty |
| Product/operations | Problem framing, prioritization, decision or process artifact, cross-functional contribution |
| Design | Work samples, interaction or system details, decision rationale, research and accessibility evidence |
| Marketing/writing | Audience/brief, authored work, experiment or editorial role, defensible outcomes |
| Leadership | Decisions, operating mechanisms, organizational scope, contribution and outcome attribution |

These are options, not mandatory checklists. Do not manufacture a project merely to fill a proof menu.

## Presentation and privacy

Make identity, relevant selected work, contact, and resume routes easy to find. Give samples meaningful labels. Check mobile readability and ordinary navigation; do not hide essential evidence behind animation, hover-only interaction, or a QR code.

Never publish restricted work, customer data, private prompts, employer source code, or internal materials without permission. Sanitization must preserve truth and respect the original sharing boundary; changing names alone does not establish permission. Offer a permitted private or public alternative where possible.

## Review

First ask whether a reviewer can identify the role and strongest evidence without opening a sample. Then open the lead sample: can the reviewer distinguish the problem, the candidate's role, important choices, output, and supported result?

Check live links and destination quality when tools allow it. Do not claim inspection of inaccessible work. Treat public portfolio examples and practitioner advice as inspiration to test, not universal hiring rules or guaranteed callback tactics.
