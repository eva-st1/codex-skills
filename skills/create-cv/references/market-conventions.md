# Market and language choices

Employer instructions, applicable requirements, and explicit candidate preferences control the output. If they conflict, identify the concrete conflict instead of silently replacing the user's choice. Market custom is a default to consider, not a legal requirement.

## Portable default

Use the vacancy language, reverse chronology, recognizable headings, and a readable primary layout. One or two pages is often a useful working target, but evidence and actual employer limits control the choice. Do not force every career into a single page.

Include only information needed for identification, contact, relevance, or eligibility. Omit optional photographs, birth details, full street addresses, family status, identity-document numbers, and referee contact details by default. Respect explicit candidate choices and lawful application requirements; do not add sensitive details merely because an old template contains them.

## United Kingdom

Use British English when appropriate to the employer and call the document a CV. Follow anonymized-application instructions exactly. The [National Careers Service](https://nationalcareers.service.gov.uk/careers-advice/cv-sections) recommends clear structure, tailoring, and excluding age, birth date, marital status, and nationality. Keep reference contacts off the CV. Page strategy remains role- and employer-specific.

## United States

Use `resume` or `résumé`, American English, and the requested page size/format. Keep the ordinary professional resume focused on job-related evidence with minimal personal data. Federal applications are a separate professional variant: read the actual announcement and current [USAJOBS Help Center](https://usajobshelp.opm.gov/hc/en-us) before stating required fields or page limits. Do not reuse historical federal-resume rules from memory.

## Poland

Use the vacancy language and natural professional terminology with correct diacritics. Preserve official foreign titles and add a clear translation when helpful. Do not automatically add a photograph or a blanket RODO consent clause. [UODO's recruitment guidance](https://uodo.gov.pl/pl/701/4471), checked 2026-10-20, says ordinary recruitment data specified in the Labour Code does not need consent and a photograph is not required. Specific purposes or additional data can change the analysis: check current guidance and any employer wording rather than inventing a universal legal clause.

## Norway

Use the vacancy language unless another language is accepted or requested. [NAV guidance](https://arbeidsplassen.nav.no/slik-skriver-du-en-god-cv) emphasizes tailoring, recent experience first, accurate language levels, and relevant qualifications. Consider trade certificates, authorizations, courses, driving licences, or volunteering when relevant. NAV examples include fuller personal details; the privacy-minimizing default here is a deliberate editorial choice, not a claim that NAV omits them.

## Switzerland

Use the vacancy language and appropriate Swiss terminology/orthography. [Swiss federal guidance](https://www.arbeit.swiss/de/stellensuchende/bewerbungstipps) describes a dossier with a motivation letter, CV, and supporting certificates; its CV guidance allows up to three pages. Submit the requested documents rather than assuming every application requires a full dossier. Traditional personal-data or photo conventions do not justify adding them without candidate choice.

## Other markets and Europass

Research the actual destination when it materially changes requirements or personal-data choices. Use Europass when requested or suited to the application channel, not as a universal European default. Do not claim a foreign qualification has an official local equivalence without support.

Sources and access limitations are recorded in [source policy](sources.md). Recheck legal and platform details for consequential decisions; an access date does not freeze requirements.
