# AI recruiting and evidence of AI-assisted work

## Keep claims current and bounded

ATS parsing, semantic matching, and hiring decisions are different processes. Check vendor documentation and the actual employer's disclosures before describing its system. Never infer hidden thresholds or model behavior from a rejection. If the platform is unknown, improve evidence clarity and extraction rather than claiming platform-specific optimization.

For example, [Greenhouse's Talent Matching FAQ](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/41131886674075-Talent-Matching-FAQ), checked 2026-10-20 and updated 2026-10-05, describes recruiter-defined weighted criteria and related skill terms. It does not treat more synonymous terms as automatically stronger evidence, and describes matching as assistive with human hiring decisions. This does not establish how another employer or product behaves.

## Clarify qualifications

For an important requirement, identify the ordinary term, supporting experience, dates/recency, contribution, and a proof link if available. Make the qualification explicit when supported, and describe partial evidence honestly. Keep official titles accurate and add a functional descriptor only where justified.

Compare the CV with supplied professional profiles, screening answers, and samples for contradictory facts. Do not require profile access to begin. Avoid keyword flooding, copied vacancy prose without evidence, hidden text, manipulated metadata, and prompt injection. Do not use an AI-text detector as a factual gate.

## Show actual AI work

Treat a named AI tool as a method rather than self-authenticating expertise. Use a qualitative evidence ladder:

- **Exposure:** tried a tool; usually too weak for a material capability claim.
- **Directed use:** completed a bounded task and can explain review and corrections.
- **Repeatable workflow:** supplied reusable context, constraints, and checks.
- **Delivered work:** produced a real artifact or change with inspectable validation and a supported output.
- **Improved practice:** evaluated, governed, monitored, or improved the workflow from evidence.

This is an editorial classification, not a credential or externally recognized proficiency scale. Use only demonstrated levels. The same method can describe Codex, another coding agent, or a noncoding AI workflow without assuming particular product features.

For a meaningful claim, identify the problem, tool/surface actually used, task context, human decisions, verification, artifact, supported result, and limitations. A tool-assisted artifact is not proof that the candidate understood or owned every step.

A defensible wording pattern is:

> Used [tool] to produce [supported artifact], supplying [context/constraints] and verifying the result with [actual checks].

Add adoption, time savings, production use, or quality improvements only when supported. Do not let tool enthusiasm displace stronger occupational evidence.

## Employer rules and privacy

Follow disclosed assessment and AI-assistance rules. Do not misrepresent authorship or conceal prohibited assistance. Keep job-related information explicit and private material protected.

Candidate rights and automated-decision rules vary by jurisdiction and process. If the task asks about a right, opt-out, or legal obligation, consult current official guidance and the employer's notice. Do not assume an opt-out exists or will improve hiring outcomes.
