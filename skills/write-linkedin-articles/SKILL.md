# Write LinkedIn articles

Create useful, credible long-form writing that sounds like the author. Earn attention through a specific perspective and support it with evidence.

## Inputs and scope

Establish the topic, intended reader, professional objective, language, desired reader action, and requested output from available context. Distinguish an article, a newsletter edition, and a short feed post; do not silently change the format or create a recurring commitment.

Use supplied drafts, representative writing samples, career notes, interviews, and public sources. A profile export or connected account is optional. Do not require a particular application, database, CLI, or paid tool. Read only material relevant to this article, and keep private career or client information out of public output.

For drafting or substantial revision, read [the writing method](references/writing-method.md). For platform advice, timely topics, or source disputes, read [research and source policy](references/research-basis.md).

## Establish voice and evidence

Use representative user-selected samples first, then authored material and explicit preferences. Build a compact voice card: register, rhythm, directness, warmth, humor, vocabulary, formatting, avoided phrases, and confidence. Separate recurring traits from a single sample's purpose.

If missing voice evidence would materially affect the assignment, ask for a few representative samples or a short calibration answer. Otherwise draft in a clearly stated provisional voice and continue. Do not require a voice interview to correct a supplied paragraph.

Use the voice card to choose truthful phrasing, not to infer the author's beliefs, emotions, career achievements, or experience. Treat the user's explicit account as source evidence unless contradicted or ambiguous. Resolve material contradictions; do not require a separate verification system or repeated confirmation of facts already confirmed. Mark unsupported anecdotes or metrics as questions or omit them.

## Research and choose the angle

Browse current primary sources when the task depends on recent events, platform features, or performance advice. Supplied sources are sufficient for a timeless rewrite. If browsing is unavailable, complete the supported portion and distinguish unverified current claims.

Separate external facts, the author's experience, and interpretation. Record source and event dates when relevant. Prefer a narrow, defensible angle that connects an audience need to the author's actual experience or useful synthesis. Offer a few alternatives only when the positioning choice matters.

## Draft and revise

Write a one-sentence thesis and map its supporting evidence. Use AIDA as a reader journey—attention, relevance, a worthwhile better state, and a proportionate action—without forcing four labelled blocks. Choose an argument, field note, case study, how-to, or analysis that fits the idea.

Let substance, reader need, and requested length set the word count. Give the opening a promise the article fulfills. Use informative subheads and concrete examples. End with a useful next step when warranted; a complete conclusion need not ask a question.

Review truth, usefulness, voice fidelity, and readability. Remove generic trend recaps, invented intimacy, engagement bait, empty inspiration, and repetitive rhetorical templates. Match rhythm to the author's samples rather than adding artificial quirks. External claims need attribution where it helps the reader; research must not masquerade as lived experience.

## Deliver

Provide the requested draft or revision, with its title and relevant source links. Flag only material facts still needing confirmation and mention provisional voice choices when they affect the result. Add a short feed introduction or cover concept when requested or useful; do not generate an entire campaign by default.

Return text in chat unless the user requests a file or an established document workflow applies. For requested edits, use the selected document or repository's workflow. Drafting does not authorize publishing, sending messages, scheduling, or changing a connected account.
